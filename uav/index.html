SIDEBAND // UAV & FPV LAB

Built to fly.
Built to understand.

I've been fascinated by flight since childhood: first radio-controlled aircraft and simulators, then amateur radio, and finally my own FPV builds beginning in 2018. For years I had the tools and drive but not the spare money. When I finally started building, all that time spent learning flight dynamics paid off.

These machines are where my interests in aerodynamics, electronics, fabrication, radio links, software, and real-world troubleshooting come together. Some have flown for years; others are still experiments on the bench. Both belong in the story.

01 / MULTIROTOROwned · flown

7-inch freestyle cruiser

My favorite heavyweight FPV cruiser: oversized motors, an analog video setup, and a flight personality that can shift between aggressive 6S freestyle and relaxed, endurance-oriented cruising. With bi-blade props and a pair of matching 4S packs in parallel, I've seen approximately 25-minute cruises. A brief experiment with DJI digital video ultimately sent me back to analog.

More than flying

It's a systems integration laboratory with propellers.

Years of simulator practice gave me the intuition to fly; building aircraft forced me to connect it to reality. An FPV system is mechanical design, power electronics, firmware, radio propagation, user controls, human factors, and field troubleshooting—all in one small machine.

That mindset also runs through my Watchtower ADS-B receiver and Artemis local AI lab. They are separate projects today, with interesting possibilities for future sensor and radio-data integrations.

My earlier amateur radio experience also shaped how I think about antennas, links, and the people who pass technical knowledge down.
